Welcome to Democratic Social Network

Social Networks should be democratic. Currently they are not, they are highly autocratic with people like emperor Zuckerberg at the top overtly censoring Facebook according to his own personal social engineering goals. Likewise, admins of groups tend to be autocrats, they are not elected or accountable in any way, can delete or block any post, comment or member with no consequences. Group members are not involved in creating or enforcing group rules. In some cases you might have responsible leaders, but the website does little or nothing to encourage this. They're called social networks but let's face it, they're pretty anti-social and actually outright misanthropic. Cyber-bullying is rampant with little meaningful means of keeping it in check except in more extreme cases where police might get involved.

We deserve better, we deserve a democratic social network that actually fosters communities in a real meaningful way. One that doesn't promote low self esteem and social anxiety. One where you don't have to worry about being cancelled or ostracized for saying things that threaten powerful people, where such criticism is actively encouraged. We need a social media platform where people can actually have vigorous debates.

A Note on the Groups You're Already Part Of

None of what follows is meant as an attack on existing community groups or the people who run them. The reality is that there are many genuine grassroots organisations full of people who care deeply about their communities and are doing their best with the resources and structures they have. The formal democratic structure of a group is only one part of what makes it effective. The character and values of the people who make it up can matter just as much — or more. A theoretically perfect direct democracy staffed by cynical or indifferent people may do less good than a more oligarchic group led by people who are genuinely socially conscious and competent. Institutions are only as good as the people inside them, and some of those people are extraordinary.

That said, we believe that the smartest and most durable decisions for the common good are made when power is genuinely diffuse — when the people most affected by a decision, or with the most relevant expertise, are the ones who have meaningful input into it. This is not just an abstract principle: it is a recognition that everybody carries some form of valuable knowledge, whether from lived experience, professional skill, or deep familiarity with their own community. Concentrating power in a small group, however well-intentioned, means that knowledge goes unused and perspectives go unheard. Easier said than done, certainly — but we think most people, across most political traditions, would agree that this is the ideal to aim for.

This website is an experiment. It is an attempt to take the problem of organisational subversion seriously, to expose the tactics used against grassroots movements, and to build something that actively counteracts them — not just in theory but in practice. That will require ongoing self-criticism and a willingness to change. We are not presenting a finished model of what the ideal democratic organisation looks like. We are trying to find out how close it is actually possible to get, in the real world, with real people. That can only be discovered through experience.

Taking Democracy to the Next Level

Traditional democracy is slow, expensive, and structurally biased toward the wealthy. Running for office — even at a local level — demands enormous amounts of time, money, and social capital. The result is a political class dominated by people who can afford to participate: those with flexible schedules, disposable income, and professional networks. Working people, carers, and anyone living paycheck to paycheck are effectively priced out of meaningful political engagement before a single vote is cast.

Technology changes this. This platform is designed around a model of highly responsive, real-time democracy — one where members can vote on any issue at any time, from any device, in seconds. There are no campaign seasons, no expensive hustings, no barriers to entry. Leadership is not awarded to whoever can run the most exhausting and costly campaign. It is awarded — and revoked — by the collective will of the membership, continuously and immediately.

That last point matters enormously: any elected admin can be recalled at any time. If a leader loses the confidence of the membership — whether through poor decisions, inactivity, or suspected bad faith — members do not have to wait for the next scheduled election. They can act now. This single feature shifts the power relationship fundamentally: leaders serve at the pleasure of the membership on an ongoing basis, not just at election time.

This is not a weakening of democracy — it is democracy taken seriously. The tools exist. The only thing that has been missing is the willingness to use them.

Why Democratic Structure Matters: The History of Socialist Subversion

Autocratic online spaces aren't just unpleasant — they are structurally easy to capture and control. A detailed analysis, The Captive Vanguard: CIA & Trotskyism, documents how intelligence agencies have historically neutralised left-wing and activist movements through three proven tactics: recruiting individuals as paid assets, planting informants throughout existing organisations, and — in the most extreme cases — building controlled opposition groups from scratch. Drawing on declassified FBI and CIA files, the Church Committee congressional investigations, and open court records, it shows how the Socialist Workers Party was penetrated by over 300 documented FBI informants by the late 1970s, how the Congress for Cultural Freedom covertly funnelled CIA money into intellectual journals to redirect radical energy away from organising and into endless theoretical disputes, and how key figures moved directly from Trotskyist leadership into CIA operations.

The structural lesson is directly relevant here: democratic accountability, elected leadership, and transparent rules are not just political ideals — they are the most effective practical defences against manufactured division and outside control. A platform where admins are elected and answerable, where decisions are made collectively, and where no single figure can arbitrarily silence dissent is far harder to subvert than one built around unchecked authority. Our Commitment: From Awareness to Active Defence

There is a telling difference between a genuine grassroots organisation and a co-opted one in how they handle the subject of infiltration and subversion. A co-opted group will acknowledge that subversion happens — they may even discuss it at length — but they will quietly shut down any serious conversation about building an effective strategy against it. They will let the topic sit as an abstract historical fact, never translating it into concrete structural defences. This is not accidental. Mere acknowledgement without action serves elite interests perfectly: it creates a climate of fear and paranoia, a corrosive sense that nobody can be trusted, that any organising effort is probably already compromised. The result is demoralisation and inaction — which is exactly what a surveillance state wants from a movement it cannot directly destroy.

A genuine organisation treats subversion as an ongoing practical problem to be solved, not a conversation-stopper. That means making education about these tactics a core part of membership, not an afterthought. It means actively encouraging members to read the primary sources — the Church Committee reports, the COINTELPRO files, the court records from the Gelfand case — and to think critically about how these methods apply today. It means building strategy collectively, in the open, with full membership participation, rather than leaving "security" to a leadership clique that members are expected to trust blindly.

You don't need to trust us.

That is not a throwaway line — it is the whole point. The question "but what if the leadership of this platform is compromised?" has a structural answer, not a personal one. You don't need to trust any individual admin or founder. What matters is whether the system's checks and balances make it possible for compromised individuals to steer the group undetected and unchallenged. On this platform, admins are elected by members and subject to removal. No single person can silence debate, delete critical posts, or block members without accountability. Decisions that affect the community are made through referenda that every member can see and vote on. A state asset who managed to get elected would find themselves unable to do what state assets actually need to do — shut down inconvenient conversations, manufacture divisions, and protect the platform's autocratic structure from democratic challenge. The structure itself is the defence.

We encourage every member to treat this not as a promise but as a hypothesis to test. Read the history. Learn the tactics. Watch how this platform responds when its own structures are challenged or questioned. A platform that welcomes that scrutiny and builds it into its culture is one that takes the problem seriously. One that deflects it does not.

We strongly recommend supplementing this voting app with regular face-to-face meetings. Digital platforms — however democratically structured — are limited. In-person gatherings build the trust, relationships, and shared context that make democratic participation meaningful, and they are significantly harder to monitor, manipulate, or disrupt than online spaces. Crucially, they also give members the opportunity to audit this app directly: members can compare their recorded votes in person, cross-check results collectively, and verify that the platform is accurately reflecting the will of the group. No software should be trusted blindly — including this one. Regular in-person audits are the most straightforward way to catch any discrepancy between what members voted and what the app reports.
